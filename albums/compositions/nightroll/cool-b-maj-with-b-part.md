# Cool B Maj with B Part — how it was made

Forked from cool-bmaj-progression (its journal is the .md sibling
there). Eight hours, 2026-08-19, written BEFORE the planned B-part
study — the study now runs in reverse. Josh: "this is certainly the
coolest thing I've ever built."

## The form

```
Intro            1.1–2.3    B
A part           2.4–6.3    G#m → B → G#m7 → Badd9
A part again     6.4–10.3   (the cycle repeats)
Break            10.4       bass ALONE: A#–G walkdown
Deceptive A part 11.1–12.4  G#m7 → Badd9 — chords shifted OFF the
                            signature beat-4 starts onto barlines
key: A           12.4.5     down a whole step (♭VII region)
Break            13.1       bass alone: single C# pivot
B part           13.2–17.4  A6 → C#m → F#m → E7 → Am → G#m7 → Am
Turn             18.2–19.1  G#m7 → C#m(add9 ✱)
loop             19.2 → 2.4
```

## The deception (Josh, verbatim intent)

> "The break was signaling A major, but I didn't go there. And then
> the next break signaled it again and I went there."

Form-level deceptive resolution: promise, denial, fulfillment. He
named the section "Deceptive A part" without knowing the textbook
term — the concept reinvented from inside. (Glossary: demonstrated.)

## The revelation

> "Once I got that E7 and A minor and I made the melody actually do
> something, it really hit me that I have something special here."

The Am is modal mixture — A major's parallel minor — and the gut that
picked it picked the one dark chord that also neighbors G#m by a half
step, so the Am ↔ G#m7 oscillation doubles as the slide home.

## The angel and the turn it forced

> "Pulse one finally did something interesting and I just let it sit
> there in the way upper registers like an angel... but then it was so
> high that I had to figure out how to get it back to the B5. I was at
> B6, and I couldn't just drop down — it didn't sound right. That's
> when I figured I had to add the turn."

THE TURN EXISTS BECAUSE THE REGISTER DEMANDED IT. Form grown from a
voice-leading problem, not chosen from a menu. The fix chain:

1. All-descending melody — "didn't sound right." Rejected.
2. Descent moved to the BASS (the inverted-texture instinct again).
3. Eight descending notes — "wasn't enough TIME." The turn expanded.
4. Final form: the bass BOUNCES, working its way down to land on
   **A directly above the G#** — a half-step approach into home, the
   oldest gravity in music, found by ear because nothing shorter felt
   finished. The turn's last chord (C#m add9, ✱ noted in the app) is
   iv of G#m: the return engineered twice over.

## Process notes

Eight hours, many iterations. "Some ideas just popped in my head
magically and I knew they would work" alongside real false starts —
both halves of the actual composer workflow. Tools used from the same
week they shipped: track rename (triangle → Bass), attached notes
(✱ Add 9), a mid-beat key-change annotation (12.4.5), section labels
as analysis (Break / Deceptive / Turn), Save As fork with provenance
note, and the loop returning to 2.4 like the parent song.
