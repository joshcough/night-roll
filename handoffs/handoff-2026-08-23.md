## Findings

### cool-b-maj-with-b-part: bass line is a compound melody

**Context:** Josh had previously tried a voice swap on this piece (melody ↔ bass) and
found it didn't work — "it just felt weird," no explanation at the time. This session
derived why.

**Derivation path, in order:**

1. Josh's initial characterization, from memory: the bass is "consistently bouncy,
   mid moves," carries the majority of the movement in the song, and the melody is
   "very sort of boring, but it needs to be." He noted it does not sound singable.
2. Josh raised the question of how bass parts actually behave in four-part vocal
   writing, noting he's a tenor and has never sung bass.
3. Tutor stated note data from `cool-b-maj-with-b-part.notes.txt` (track 3): range
   D#2–A3; rhythm almost entirely 0.25 durations with one 1.0 duration on a strong
   beat per bar; bar 4 contains B2, B2, D#2, B2 in consecutive sixteenths; bar 10
   ends A2 A2, A#2 A#2, G2 G2. Observed that much of the apparent motion is repeated
   notes and octave drops rather than stepwise travel.
4. Tutor asked which element carries the harmony: the sustained strong-beat note or
   the sixteenth figure.
5. **Josh's answer:** the short notes act as pedal tones on chord roots (lots of
   repeated G#s and Bs); the long notes are the harmonically active ones. Predicted
   the long notes would be thirds or fifths rather than roots, without checking.
6. Tutor confirmed against the data: bar 5 short G#2 / long D#3 (fifth above); bar 4
   short B2 / long F#3; bar 13 short A2 / long E3. Consistent nearly throughout.
   Bar 15 is the exception — F#2 short, F#3 long, same pitch class at the octave.
   **Josh's prediction was correct.**
7. Tutor asked how many voices it would take to do that in a four-part chorale.
8. **Josh's answer:** two. Noted correctly that one voice suffices *mechanically*
   since the line is monophonic, but functionally the repeated notes would become
   longer bass notes and the fifths would go to the tenors.
9. Tutor named the phenomenon: **compound melody** (implied polyphony). Noted Bach's
   solo cello and violin works as the standard reference case.
10. Tutor asked whether that explained why the melody could afford to be boring.
11. **Josh's answer:** yes — the bass is "doing the work of two different people," so
    a more active melody would imply two more (soprano and alto), and with the rhythm
    track also occupying voices, "you would have parts stepping on each other."
12. Tutor named that budget: **textural density**.

**Conclusion (Josh's):** the failed voice swap failed because the line is compound.
Moving it to the top put a two-strand line where a single melodic line belongs; the
ear tried to hear one soprano and received a bass and a tenor stacked in time.

**Note:** the compound structure was not intentional. Josh: "I certainly didn't
intend anything like this. I just wrote a baseline."

## Glossary

Add at **ENCOUNTERED** (not DEMONSTRATED):

- **compound melody** (a.k.a. implied polyphony) — a single monophonic line implying
  two or more voices by alternating between registral strands.
- **textural density** — the total voice budget of a texture; a compound line
  consumes two voices' worth rather than one.

Josh's reasoning for ENCOUNTERED rather than DEMONSTRATED: he identified the
structure in his own music but did not write it deliberately, and he holds that
DEMONSTRATED implies intent.

## PROPOSED: replace the ENCOUNTERED/DEMONSTRATED binary with a two-axis scale

**Origin:** Josh, this session. The current binary has nowhere to put "identified
this in my own music but wrote it by accident," which is exactly where compound
melody landed tonight. His words: DEMONSTRATED, to him, implies conscious intent —
"I thought about something and did it on purpose." That reading is defensible, but
it leaves recognition-side progress untracked entirely.

Josh sketched a five-level scale off the top of his head and explicitly invited
refinement. On inspection his levels split across two independent axes, so the
proposal below separates them.

### Axis 1 — Recognition (analysis)

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | HEARD | Encountered once. Vaguely aware it exists. |
| R2 | RECOGNIZED | Seen several times. Can identify it when pointed at it, or with guiding questions. |
| R3 | DERIVED | Identified it unaided in music not previously analyzed. |
| R4 | FLUENT | Derives it reliably across unfamiliar contexts. |

R3 is the level added in refinement. Josh's original list had no slot for it, but it
is the entire FF1 skill — spotting a device in someone else's music with nobody
prompting. It sits above RECOGNIZED and is not production at all.

The R2/R3 line is guided vs. unaided. If a tutor asked a question that pointed at the
answer, it's R2 regardless of how good the answer was.

### Axis 2 — Production (composition)

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | — | Never used it. |
| P1 | FOUND | Used it, noticed only after the fact. |
| P2 | DEMONSTRATED | Used it once, deliberately. |
| P3 | MASTERY | Used it repeatedly and deliberately, across pieces. |

P1 is Josh's "used it once maybe by accident." It is a real and common state,
especially for someone with 30 years of playing and 1 year of theory — the hands
know things the vocabulary hasn't caught up to.

### How the axes interact

Independent. Not a ladder. Both directions occur:

- **High P, low R** — you can write something repeatedly without being able to name
  or spot it elsewhere. Probably the default state for most of Josh's existing
  guitar vocabulary.
- **High R, low P** — you can analyze a device thoroughly and never have used it.
  Expected for most of the chorale material in the curriculum.

So each glossary term carries two coordinates, e.g. `compound melody — R2/P1`.

### Recognition-only terms

Some entries have no production axis: methodological terms (non-circular derivation),
descriptive terms (pitch class, register), and structural labels applied after the
fact. Mark these `R2/—` rather than `R2/P0`, so "not applicable" is distinguishable
from "hasn't done it yet."

### Tonight's entries under the proposed scale

| Term | Level | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| compound melody | **R2/P1** | R2: identified with guiding questions, not unaided. P1: present in `cool-b-maj-with-b-part`, written without intent. |
| textural density | **R2/—** | Reasoned to the concept unaided (step 11) before it was named, but the naming was the tutor's. Descriptive term; no production axis. |

### Migration

Existing glossary entries map cleanly: ENCOUNTERED → R1 or R2 (judgment call per
term, Josh's), DEMONSTRATED → R3 or higher on recognition, plus whatever production
level applies. Retrofit is probably not worth doing exhaustively — apply the new
scale going forward and convert entries as they come up in session.
