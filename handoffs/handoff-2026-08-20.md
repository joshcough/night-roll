### 1. Rebar — the whole intro moved two beats earlier

**His reason, stated:** he wanted the next section "to start on a bar,
on the beginning of a bar and not in it a bit."

Consequence he may not have been optimizing for but got anyway: G4–D5

### 2. The hang he thought he couldn't get

Earlier in the session he tried sustaining F♯, then G, then A♯, then
C♯ into the gap, and concluded: "sadly, I can't figure it out." The
tutor's read at that point was that the material was refusing it and
silence should win. **He solved it anyway, and the solve was
rhythmic, not pitch-selection:**

- Triangle bar 5: F3 at beat 2 (one beat), then **F♯3 at beat 3 for
  1.5 beats** — the leading tone is held *longer* than the note before
  it, so the climb stretches into the seam rather than accelerating
  through it.
- Melody bar 5 ends G5 at 3.5, a quarter, releasing at 4.5.
- **Both voices release together at 4.5.** The F♯/G clash that killed
  the earlier attempts is still present but lasts one beat and ends
  simultaneously — so it reads as a crush into the gap, not a sour
  sustain hanging alone.

His description: "I extended the last two notes, and but just not a
lot, just a little bit, and it works great."

**Record this one.** The failed hypothesis was "the wrong note is being
held." The actual answer was "the right note held the wrong length,
and released at the wrong time." Worth remembering next time a sustain
refuses.

### 4. Tempo: 240 at bar 6 — triple, not double

### OPEN — 240-eighths vs 120-sixteenths

Josh raised it himself and deliberately deferred: "I could just make
that decision later if for some reason it makes it easier to write the
music."

Identical in sound — same onsets, same speed, zero audible difference.
What differs is the working environment: bar length (1s vs 2s), what
bar.beat addresses mean for chord bands / sections / loop targets,
which snap grid the fast material needs, and how it engraves.

The deciding factor named: **whether anything in the fast section moves
SLOWER than the bass pulse.** Long chords or a sustained melody at 240
become walls of ties; 120 gives room. Conversely if it goes the metal
route he floated — "launch right into a solo with tons of sixteenth
notes" — the melody is *faster* than the bass, sixteenths-at-240 is
32nd territory, and 120 is what keeps the notation and the grid sane.

He has no melody yet and correctly refused to pre-solve it. **Leave at
240. Revisit once something is written over the G2 hammer.** Cheap to
change: the notes don't move in time, only their labels do.

## Tooling — ruler selection has no snapping, and it is the loudest complaint

Reported with real heat on 2026-08-21. Verbatim: *"Selecting an area in
the ruler fucking sucks now. It is so hard to click exactly on a bar. I
feel like I'm always off by a sixteenth note either way. I can't

His stated rule: *"If I'm clicking near a whole bar measure, it should be

**This supersedes the existing open item** that describes ruler drag
snapping *to eighth-note boundaries*. Eighths are too fine to fix this —
he is missing by roughly a sixteenth, so an eighth-note grid would still
let him land wrong. **Bar boundaries are the unit he actually works in
when selecting ranges** (loop targets, section selection, analysis
ranges are all bar-addressed).

Suggested shape, not signed off:
- **Snap ruler drag endpoints to bar lines by default.** Magnetic, with
  a generous catch radius — the failure mode to avoid is a near-miss,
  not a coarse grab.
- The catch radius should be defined in **screen pixels, not musical
  time**, so it stays usable at every zoom level. A sixteenth is a very
  different number of pixels zoomed in vs out.
- Finer granularity should be **opt-in** (modifier key, zoom level, or
  an explicit toggle) rather than the default.
- Note this is Apple Pencil input on an iPad — pointer precision is
  worse than mouse, and he is not switching to a mouse (it hurts his
  hands). Tolerances tuned for a mouse will feel broken here.

Worth asking him: when zoomed in far enough that bars are wide, does he
still want bar snapping, or does he expect it to loosen automatically?

## Tooling — ruler clicks destroy the note selection

Reported 2026-08-21, same session as the ruler snapping item, and it
compounds it: *"when I... well, I saw something, and then I click in the
ruler, the fucking lasso disappears. And then I have to relasso the notes
just because I wanted to move the ruler."*

**The bug:** interacting with the ruler clears the current note
selection. So adjusting a loop range or playhead after lassoing costs him
the lasso, and he has to rebuild it.

**Why it's worse than it sounds:** these two surfaces are used *together*.
The workflow is select notes → set the range around them → audition. And
because ruler clicking is currently imprecise (see item above), he often
needs **several attempts** at the ruler — each one destroying the
selection again.

Suggested shape, not signed off:
- **Ruler interaction and note selection should be independent state.**
  Clicking the ruler sets a time range or playhead; it should not touch
  which notes are selected.
- Generalize before fixing narrowly: audit what *else* clears the note
  selection as an incidental side effect. Selection is expensive to
  rebuild by Pencil, so anything that discards it should do so only on
  explicit intent (click on empty roll background, Escape, new lasso).
- Deselect-on-background-click is presumably intentional and should
  stay — the ruler is simply not the background.
